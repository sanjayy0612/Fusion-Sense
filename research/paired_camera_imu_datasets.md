# Paired camera + IMU datasets for FusionSense

Research date: 2026-08-13

## Recommendation

Use **UP-Fall** as the principal pretraining dataset for the FusionSense fusion layer. It is the only verified candidate below that directly contains paired camera data and 3-axis accelerometer + gyroscope data while also covering all five intended output classes: `walking`, `standing`, `sitting`, `lying`, and `falling`.

Do **not** begin by downloading the complete 812 GB consolidated release. First download a small, matched subset from the official per-subject/per-activity/per-trial selector: one camera view plus one wearable location (preferably the belt/waist) for Subjects 1–5, Activities 1–8 and 11, and Trials 1–3. Activities 1–5 become `falling`; 6, 7, 8, and 11 map directly to `walking`, `standing`, `sitting`, and `lying`. Exclude Activities 9 (`picking up`) and 10 (`jumping`) for the first five-class experiment.

The original paper and official site describe 17 subjects, 11 activities, three trials, five body-worn IMUs, two cameras, timestamps, and synchronized/tagged files. The raw consolidated dataset is reported as 812 GB and the feature dataset as 171 GB, so selective acquisition is essential. Sources: [original paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/s19091988), [official dataset site](https://sites.google.com/up.edu.mx/har-up/), [official competition description](https://sites.google.com/up.edu.mx/challenge-up-2019/data).

## Candidate comparison

| Dataset | Verified paired modalities and scale | Fit to five labels | Access and rights | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UP-Fall** | Two RGB cameras; five wearable units, each with 3-axis accelerometer and 3-axis gyroscope; 17 subjects; 11 activities; three trials. The site says acquired data are synchronized and tagged. | Exact mapping for all five labels. Five distinct falls can be collapsed to `falling`. | Public official selector, but complete raw data are approximately 812 GB. The official page states copyright by Universidad Panamericana but does not display a standard reusable-data license; obtain written confirmation before redistribution or commercial use. | **Best match.** Use a selective subset first. |
| **C-MHAD** | Simultaneous RGB webcam at 15 fps and waist/wrist 3-axis acceleration + angular velocity at 50 Hz; timestamp synchronization; 12 subjects; 240 two-minute continuous streams (8 hours). Its transition subset includes stand, sit, lie and fall transitions. | Useful supplementary data for `sitting`/`standing`/`lying`/`falling`, but no verified `walking` class in the transition subset and labels are transitions rather than stable postures. | Public download and reader code from the University of Texas at Dallas page. No explicit standardized license was found on the official page; clarify reuse terms. | Best small continuous-data supplement, not a replacement for UP-Fall. |
| **MMAct** | RGB, keypoints, acceleration, gyroscope, orientation, Wi-Fi and pressure; over 1,900 untrimmed videos / 36k clips; 37 classes; 20 subjects in the original ICCV paper (the current project page advertises a later 40-subject version); timestamps/toolkit support merging sensors sampled at different rates. | Official examples verify `walking` and `standing`, but the available primary-source pages inspected did not establish an exact five-label mapping, especially `lying` and falls. | Dataset is supplied after a Google request form. Academic research only; commercial use and redistribution, including modified data, are prohibited. | Valuable large-scale cross-modal pretraining only after its label list is checked; restrictive terms matter. |
| **UTD-MHAD** | RGB, depth, skeleton and one wearable 3-axis accelerometer + gyroscope at 50 Hz; 8 subjects, 27 actions, four repetitions (861 valid sequences). | Has walking-in-place, sit-to-stand and stand-to-sit, but no verified lying or falling classes. Sensor moves between wrist and thigh according to action, creating a placement mismatch. | Public-domain dataset as stated in its original paper; roughly 2 GB according to the CVF dataset catalog. | Useful for validating fusion code, weak semantic match for the final classifier. |
| **KFall** | Lower-back 9-axis IMU at 100 Hz; 32 male subjects; 21 ADLs, 15 simulated fall types; video was synchronized for temporal labeling at up to 90 Hz. | Strong fall/ADL IMU corpus, but does not directly supply the desired stable five-class paired training set. | Official paper says sensor files, labels and **demo videos** are public. It does not establish that every synchronized source video is downloadable for visual-model training. | Use for IMU/fall encoder evaluation, not as the primary paired fusion dataset unless full videos are confirmed. |
| **Berkeley MHAD** | Temporally synchronized/calibrated multicamera, depth, motion capture, six accelerometers and audio; 12 subjects, 11 actions, five repetitions (~660 sequences, ~82 minutes). | General action set; not a verified five-posture/fall mapping and the wearable modality is accelerometer-only, not accelerometer + gyroscope. | Dataset mirrors exist, but the original UC Berkeley host is no longer a dependable acquisition path and clear reuse terms were not verified. | Lower priority. |

## Exact UP-Fall acquisition and preprocessing path

1. Open the [official UP-Fall site](https://sites.google.com/up.edu.mx/har-up/) and use its per-subject/activity/trial download selector. The dynamic Google-hosted links were not extractable in this research session; if the selector fails, contact the corresponding authors through the [paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/s19091988). Avoid unverified mirrors.
2. Pilot only `S1..S5`, `A1..A8,A11`, `T1..T3`, `Camera1`, and the synchronized `Data Set` CSV for each trial. Add Camera2 only after the pipeline works. This keeps the subject/activity/trial identity paired without attempting the 812 GB full acquisition.
3. From each trial CSV select the belt IMU columns: accelerometer columns 16–18 and angular-velocity columns 19–21, with timestamp in column 1. The official page documents ankle (2–7), pocket (9–14), belt (16–21), neck (23–28), and wrist (30–35); keep the body location in metadata.
4. Extract MediaPipe Pose landmarks from each camera frame. Preserve the original frame filename/timestamp and add detection confidence; do not discard failed frames silently.
5. Align each pose frame to IMU time by nearest timestamp within half an IMU period, or resample both modalities onto an 18 Hz common grid. The UP-Fall system is reported at approximately 18 Hz. Record the absolute time error for every match and discard unmatched points rather than aligning by row number.
6. Construct 2–3 second windows (36–54 time steps at 18 Hz), using the same start/end time for both modalities. Normalize pose around the hip midpoint and torso scale. Normalize IMU channels using statistics computed from the training subjects only.
7. Map labels as `{A1..A5: falling, A6: walking, A7: standing, A8: sitting, A11: lying}`. For falls, consider labeling only windows intersecting the actual fall/impact interval; otherwise the 10-second fall trials contain pre-fall standing and post-fall lying that can teach contradictory labels.
8. Split by **subject**, never by random windows: for the five-subject pilot use S1–S3 train, S4 validation, S5 test. For the full dataset, reserve entire unseen subjects for validation/test. Keep both camera views from a subject in the same split.
9. Cache one record per aligned window, for example `{subject, activity, trial, camera, imu_location, t0, t1, pose[T,33,4], imu[T,6], mask_pose[T], label}`. Train frozen/pretrained modality encoders first, then cross-attention/fusion and classifier; only later fine-tune encoder top layers with a smaller learning rate.
10. After public-data pretraining, collect a small synchronized FusionSense dataset using the actual ESP32/MPU-6050 placement and laptop camera. Fine-tune on it because UP-Fall's cameras, IMU hardware, 18 Hz rate, young actors and controlled laboratory differ from deployment.

## Small derived MediaPipe skeleton release: important warning

The [3D Skeletons UP-Fall release](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.12773013) is only about 4 MB and contains MediaPipe-style 33-joint CSVs for five subjects, two cameras and matching `C{camera}S{subject}A{activity}T{trial}` filenames. It is CC BY 4.0 and is useful for quickly testing the pose branch.

It is **not yet defensible to fuse those rows directly with original UP-Fall IMU rows**. The Zenodo description says sequences under 100 extracted frames were removed and some files are missing; it documents joint columns plus an impact label but does not establish that original camera timestamps/frame indices were preserved. The companion GitHub README mentions a timestamp, creating a metadata inconsistency. The shared subject/activity/trial filename proves trial identity, not row-level temporal alignment. Use it for pose-only prototyping unless inspection of an actual CSV confirms an original timestamp/frame identifier that can be matched to the UP-Fall sensor timestamps. Never join it to IMU merely by row number or normalized sequence position.

Sources: [Zenodo record and CC BY 4.0 license](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.12773013), [authors' repository and naming scheme](https://github.com/Tresor-Koffi/3D_skeletons-UP-Fall-Dataset).

## Known limitations

- UP-Fall contains staged falls from healthy people aged 18–24 in a controlled room; it is not clinical evidence of real elderly falls.
- Its original class labels are trial-level. Exact fall-onset labels need verification or manual annotation to prevent pre/post-event label noise.
- Sensor placement/domain shift is substantial. Match the selected UP-Fall wearable position to the intended FusionSense device location, then fine-tune on locally collected paired data.
- Rights are not equivalent across datasets. UP-Fall's official page does not expose a standard license; MMAct explicitly prohibits commercial use and redistribution; the small derived skeleton dataset is CC BY 4.0.
